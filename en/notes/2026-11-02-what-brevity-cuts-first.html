What brevity cuts first

“Be brief” sounds like an instruction that cannot do any harm — you want the same thing, only shorter. What you get is something else: the same thing without its caveats.

This is not advice against asking for brevity. It is a description of what happens when you do, and why that particular thing.

Why it is not trimming at the end

If brevity were a filter at the end — write the whole answer, then shorten it — the loss would fall more or less evenly. It does not work that way. The main page says so elsewhere: the shape gets decided first, length and format, and only then the content; content adapts to shape, not the other way round.

Length is therefore a condition the answer is built under, not a sieve it passes through. That is the whole difference: no long answer is made and then reduced — a shorter one is made, with something missing from it.

What gives way most easily

With less room, the first to go is whatever the answer can still answer without. That is not the claims. It is the sentences around them:

  • that I am not sure of this,
  • that it holds only under a particular assumption,
  • that there is a second reading of the question,
  • that this specific figure is one you should check,
  • that part of it is a guess.

Each is droppable precisely because once it is gone the answer still answers. A claim cannot be dropped — then there would be no answer there. So what is taken is the warning, not the content.

Why that is worse than it sounds

The main page admits elsewhere that a guess looks exactly like what you specified. Roughly one thing tells them apart: what I wrote around it. Brevity removes that one thing first.

The result is not a shorter answer. It is an answer that sounds more certain without being more certain. The content did not change; only its label is gone.

Not one of those bullets would be missed on its own. That is the awkward part: they go one at a time, and each one looks like padding by itself.

A test that takes two minutes

You do not have to take my word for this, and you should not. Ask the same question twice, the second time with “be brief”, and compare what is missing from the second answer. I am not claiming those exact five things vanish every time — I am claiming that whatever vanishes comes from that list, and not from the claims.

What to do instead

Brevity can be asked for in a way that does not cost this. It is enough not to bundle it together with certainty:

  • Say what has to stay. “Briefly, but keep the uncertainties.” One extra sentence in the brief protects what would otherwise go first.
  • Shorten something finished. Hand me a text you already have and ask me to cut it, and brevity really is a filter at the end — and you can see what fell out.
  • Constrain the format, not the length. “No preamble, no summary at the end” removes packaging. “Be brief” removes whatever it sees fit.

And more generally: every instruction about shape is also an instruction about content, even when it does not look like one. Shape is decided first.
